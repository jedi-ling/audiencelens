How a judgment is produced, constrained and verified

AudienceLens puts every judgment through a traceable pipeline, and every step of it can be read line by line in the open-source repo.

// 01 produce
How a judgment is
produced
◇

Independent review, in groups

72 behavior-driven personas are split into packs and write their reviews independently, so nobody is anchored to anyone else's opinion up front.

⇄

Cross-examination on the top 3 disagreements

The system picks the three points the panel splits on hardest and has each side rebut the other once, against the original arguments. Whatever everyone already agreed on is filtered out.

⚖

Vote changes are decided by code

Who actually moved after the rebuttal is decided by code comparing stance and confidence before and after, and the threshold lives in an open-source config file.

// 02 constrain
How a judgment is
constrained
⌘

Three-level evidence tags + an inference ceiling

Every conclusion carries its evidence level: direct (observed in your material) / inferred (reasonable inference) / gap (missing information). More than 40% inferred and the finding is flagged as over-inference.

⊘

Ethics guardrails

Identity is decoupled from behavior: personas are defined by behavioral traits, and the gender / age / region fields are stripped before anything is injected into the prompt. A separate check flags identity-to-behavior causal reasoning in the output.

// 03 verify
How a judgment is
verified
⏱

Predictions on the record · real outcomes filled back in

When a review finishes, every prediction is timestamped and stored. Once you have run the real playtest you fill the actual outcome back in, and the system compares prediction against reality.

▲

Per-persona hit rate

After enough reviews accumulate you can look up how right each persona has been. When the sample is too small it is marked low_confidence.

What you get is a traceable record of judgments: predictions, filled-back outcomes and hit rates are all queryable and exportable. The system does not reweight personas by their track record on its own — whether to trust one of them more is your call.

FAQ

Are 72 personas enough?

Why 72 personas instead of hundreds?

Because a persona covers a behavioral decision pattern, not a demographic slice — “price-sensitive buyer” is an entire class of “opaque pricing means no purchase” logic defined on 12 behavioral axes, not one individual. For reference: a human focus group runs 6–12 people per session. Inflating the count is actively harmful: behavioral distance between personas shrinks, stances converge, and the disagreement signal gets diluted — and disagreement is exactly what fuels our cross-examination. We add packs by gap (vertical domains, regional culture), not to round up a number.

Only a few personas speak in each review — where are the rest?

The panel selector assembles a panel from the matching persona pack per task type; deep review runs 3/6/8 personas — the same scale as a human focus group, a methodological choice rather than a shortcut. With two-tier review enabled, the whole selected pack gets a cheap triage pass first, and a deterministic gate picks the deep-review panel by disagreement signal — who advanced and why is itemized in the report.

How do you prove the panel is accurate?

Not by claiming it. Every report’s predictions are timestamped and archived; after a real playtest you record the outcomes, and the system accumulates per-persona hit rates and confidence calibration (Brier score). With small samples it says low_confidence out loud — we don’t turn 3 samples into a “100% accuracy” banner.
